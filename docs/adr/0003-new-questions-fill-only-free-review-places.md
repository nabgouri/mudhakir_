# New Questions fill only the places the day's reviews leave free

Each Review Session asks 6 Questions, five times a day, so about 30 a day. The ma'had posts about 7 Lessons a week, and one Lesson gave 31 Questions. Each new Question comes back several times over the following weeks, and daily reviews settle at roughly 7–10 times the number of new Questions added each day. So 30 places sustain only a few new Questions a day. A fixed cap of 10 new a day would let the unseen queue grow without end.

So a new Question is introduced only into a place left free once that session's due reviews are asked. They come in this order:

1. The newest Lesson's Core Questions.
2. Older Lessons' Core Questions, oldest first.
3. Detail Questions.

Due reviews come first in a session, and the session alternates between Subjects (interleaving).

The routine writes small Questions (the minimum information principle). A division or list gives one Question per part, plus a Core Question asking for the parts by name. «لماذا» and «ما الفرق» Questions are written only where the material itself states the reason or the difference. Invented scenarios are never written, since in Fiqh a made-up case can state a ruling the material doesn't.

The scheduler no longer uses ts-fsrs's 1-minute and 10-minute learning steps for Good or Easy. Prayers are hours apart, so a 10-minute step meant every new Question came back at the very next prayer, and the same few Questions repeated at every salat. A Question rated Again or Hard still comes back at the next prayer.

## Considered Options

- **Fewer Questions per Lesson (Core only):** keeps the load low, but loses the details for good.
- **Bigger sessions (10 per prayer):** more places, but longer than a moment after the prayer.
- **Newest Lesson first for every Question:** older Lessons' Questions would never be reached.

## Consequences

- A busy week fills sessions with reviews, and its Detail Questions wait; nothing is dropped.
- Each Question carries a Core or Detail mark, which the routine sets.
